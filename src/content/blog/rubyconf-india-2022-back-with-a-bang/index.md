Ruby is such a beautiful, artful language that it's easy to see why its enthusiasts love to meet in person to discuss its application. RubyConf 2022 — the 11th edition of the annual Ruby conference in India — was back after a two-year hiatus and hosted speakers from all around the globe to share their prowess on Ruby.

This was my first ever RubyConf and let me just start by saying RubyConf was, well… awesome! (Just wanted to get that out of the way early.)

Held on the 27th of August, it was a one-day single-track event filled with great talks, great people, and great parties. The venue, at the picturesque Hyatt Regency in Pune, was perfect.

It also marked the first RubyConf without Satish Talim, and the conference commenced with the community paying homage to Satish Manohar Talim. The organizers of RubyConf India promised a diverse lineup of speakers, with topics ranging from beginner-friendly how-tos to insightful presentations for pros. The keynote speech by Dinesh Kumar, CEO of RailsFactory, gave way to some amazing talks.

## Talks

It's really hard to choose my favorite talks because they were all excellent. I'd recommend watching all the videos when they're posted, but here are the ones that stood out to me:

- **Being Restful over GraphQL** — Tejas Dinkar
- **Static typing in Ruby 3 using RBS** — Gaurav Singh
- **The Achilles Heel of a Rails Developer: Upgrading a Rails App** — Rishi Jain
- **0–300 km/hr with Bullet Train** — Jim Remsik
- **DevOps Practices with GitHub** — MV Karan
- **Data Analytics the Ruby/Rails Way** — Navarasu
- **How Music Works, Using Ruby** — Thijs Cadier
- **Engineering Excellence** — Naren

## M.I.N.A.S.W.A.N

One of the biggest reasons to attend a conference that hasn't been mentioned yet is for networking. You get to meet all sorts of people who are there for more or less the same reasons you are — plus they share the same interest as you: **Ruby!** You get a chance to exchange war stories with your peers and learn a lot from some of the brightest minds in the industry. The conference presenters make themselves available too, so you can go and chat with some of these folks. Remember, this is Ruby we're talking about, with the most important principle being that *"it's all about our community"*.

## Engagement activities

In addition to the talks, there were many other engagement activities going on.

One of these was the **fast meeting session**, arranged at the start of the day where one would meet and greet their neighbours at the conference.

There were also **unconference sessions** — typically longer than talks (an hour) — ranging from topics like *"Health for Developers"* to *"Contributing to Ruby"* and *"Running a Company"*.

Another activity organised at RubyConf was **flash talks**: very short 5-minute talks anyone can present. They were really insightful and fun to attend. My teammate presented one on *Dead Code & how to eliminate it*.

There were **sponsor booths** in the hall area hosting a plethora of online quizzes, games, and contests. It's a good idea to simply go and say hi if you're interested in career opportunities or just want some sponsor swag (stickers, bookmarks, etc.). There were handsome prizes for the winners — I remember a lucky one winning an Xbox!

Apart from the coffee breaks during the day, another activity I personally loved was the **impromptu drum circle session** — a crowd participation activity to keep our brains active.

## After the conference

When the official program was over, there were still many ways to keep the conference experience going.

A nice time for extended networking was at the party after the final rounds of talks. GitHub was this year's party sponsor, and the evening was filled with people reaching out to fellow Rubyists they met during the conference — thanking them for the discussions, offering to meet again sometime, and following up on conversations they'd had.

## RubyConf perks

Now to the fun stuff. You didn't think it was all business and no fun, did you? I hope not, because at RubyConf you work hard and play hard. Cool stuff at this year's conference:

- After-parties and social gatherings
- Swag
- T-shirts! You will not need to buy any shirts for a while
- Endless supply of coffee
- Sponsor booths with quizzes and contests
- Lots of snacks
- De-lish lunches!

One might have found these cute pens and pencils with seed capsules that [Jiva](https://www.jiva.ag/) had put in the delegate kit floating around. You can plant your pen(cil)s after use — I found ones with coriander and mustard seeds!

## Wrap-up

So if you're a Ruby developer or love the Ruby programming language, I promise it's worth the price of admission. Choosing RubyConf is investing in yourself and in growing professionally. I'm lucky enough to be part of an organization that encourages us to be a part of such amazing conferences. If you can't afford the price yourself, it's probably a good idea to investigate whether your employer could help — e.g. via a yearly professional development budget. If you're a student, these conferences often have special student tickets or scholarships that are less expensive.

Finally, it was an eventful day filled with "gems" of talks, lots of learning, a plethora of fun activities, and meeting new people.
